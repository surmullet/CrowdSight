## Fixed overhead crowd-video lead: Crosswalk (Bangkok)

- **Official dataset and code page:** [Crosswalk dataset](https://github.com/Nanasaki-Ai/Crosswalk). The authors describe 120 untrimmed five-minute videos from one Bangkok intersection, captured from fixed overhead view at 1200×1100 and 30 fps across weather and time conditions. This is a useful candidate for a long-form fixed-camera diagnostic, but it is one site in Thailand, not Vietnam, and does not establish generalization to a Da Nang target camera.
- **Video package:** the authors link three Figshare parts. The first, [`video_001.avi`–`video_040.avi`](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29983897), is listed at 17.38 GB. Obtain exact sizes/hashes for the remaining parts before any transfer; do not assume that a 40-video subset can be selectively downloaded from the archive.
- **Rights review gate:** the GitHub usage statement says educational purposes only, non-commercial use, attribution required, and no redistribution. The linked first Figshare record declares CC BY 4.0. Those published terms conflict. Do not download, annotate, upload to Kaggle/SAM, or run CrowdSight inference until the dataset authors clarify in writing which terms govern the videos and expressly confirm internal noncommercial model evaluation and annotation rights. Keep any future downloaded media and labels private and outside Git.
- **Label fit:** released annotations describe pedestrian–vehicle interaction events and provide boxes for the key vehicle at event entry/exit. They are not complete per-frame pedestrian boxes and cannot directly score CrowdSight person detection or count error. A separate complete, human-reviewed pedestrian annotation set would be required. Event labels could support their documented interaction task only, not crowd density, parking occupancy, or waiting-time claims.
- **Checkpoint independence:** the complete `best.pt` training inventory remains unresolved. Even if access and annotation are approved, this is only a held-out candidate after video/source IDs and all training/pretraining ancestry are compared and reviewed. Lock the selected videos and sampled frames before inference.
- **Decision:** promising source characteristics, not approved or acquired. The terms conflict, package volume is large, and the available annotation ontology does not meet the crowd evaluation label requirement. Continue with the Da Nang controlled-access request for Vietnam image-level evaluation; use Crosswalk only if the authors resolve rights and the project can fund the required independent pedestrian annotation.

### MOT20 official benchmark (dense pedestrian evaluation candidate)

- **Official archive:** [MOT20](https://motchallenge.net/data/MOT20/). The current archive page lists four annotated training sequences and omits license terms. A historical CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 notice is not authenticated by the current source. The product lead authorized private local noncommercial evaluation; a human reviewer must authenticate terms before redistribution or rights claims.
- **Acquisition:** MOT20.zip (5,028,926,248 bytes; SHA-256 ebcf0e3d44e4f50b5357d24817e5db485d777633d1b8ca9e8380d1c8437dbdd7) and MOT20Labels.zip (19,246,343 bytes; SHA-256 95decf629c332f80ace18024bf876996e6be89908ec593f05cda000270d6d9ad) passed ZIP CRC checks and remain outside Git.
- **Evaluation:** MOT20-05 v4 was sampled every 25th frame plus the final frame: 134 frames from one training sequence. The complete sequence is the split unit; this is not the benchmark test set. The v4 manifest explicitly records current license terms as unverified, and the evaluator requires human evidence review. Results: 8,240 TP, 4,054 FP, 17,880 FN, precision 0.6702, recall 0.3155, count MAE 103.18, RMSE 105.04, bias -103.18. See [diagnostic report](../models/crowd-best-local-mot20-diagnostic.md).

- **Limits:** dense pedestrians are an external diagnostic, not Vietnam motorbike, parking, or waiting-time evidence. best.pt full lineage and sequence independence are unverified. This is not held-out evaluation, site acceptance, or release approval. Do not upload or redistribute data or derivatives without authenticated transfer rights.
- **Decision:** authorized acquisition and local exploratory evaluation are complete; rights authentication and full checkpoint-lineage review remain open. Keep all assets private.

## Vietnam fixed-CCTV data leads: access and license unresolved

- **UIT-VinaDeveS22 vehicle images:** [Trinh and Nguyen (2022)](https://doi.org/10.22144/ctu.jen.2022.042) report 1,364 CCTV-derived images from six video sources in Vietnam, covering bicycle, motorcycle, car, van, truck, bus, and fire truck classes across day/night, clear/rain, and sparse/dense conditions. The paper describes a 653/173/538 train/validation/test image split and states that some train/test images came from different video sources, but does not identify source-video IDs for a reuse audit or establish that the original videos are released. No dataset-specific reuse license or authorized download route was verified in the paper. This is a possible motorbike-detector domain lead only after access, rights, source identities, and partition ancestry are confirmed; vehicle boxes do not label pedestrians, configured parking stalls, or parking occupancy.
- **Vehicle Tracking Dataset (VnTraffic):** [Zenodo record 18195750](https://zenodo.org/records/18195750) describes a Vietnam traffic sub-dataset derived from a video published by the official HTV – Hanoi Radio and Television Station YouTube channel. The record says vehicles, including cars and motorbikes, have frame IDs, track IDs, and bounding boxes, and lists a 39.5 MB ZIP file. The Zenodo record's Rights/License field is blank. Dataset package size is not the same as the complete source-video size or permission to reuse the video. Do not download, label, redistribute, or use this as evaluation/training data until the dataset authors and upstream source rights are clarified. It does not claim pedestrian/crowd or stall-occupancy labels.
- **Da Nang CCTV traffic dataset:** [Vo, Nguyen, and Tran (2026)](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v88n1/67301/html) describe 23,364 images extracted from continuous fixed-view CCTV at Da Nang intersections, with pedestrians, bicycles, motorbikes, cars, buses, trucks, and traffic lights. The paper reports a video-level 70/10/20 split and cameras mounted 6–10 m high with downward viewing angles of 30–45 degrees. Its data availability statement says access is for non-commercial research on reasonable request under controlled access; commercial use requires explicit author permission. The article's CC BY 4.0 license applies to the article, not automatically to the controlled dataset. The [access-request brief](da-nang-cctv-access-request.md) lists the permission, annotation, and source-partition details needed before any transfer. This is an image dataset derived from videos, not an openly downloadable source-video release, so image-only access cannot support temporal tracking, parking occupancy, or waiting-time evaluation.
- **Decision:** These are potentially relevant Vietnam-domain research leads, not approved assets. None may enter the held-out benchmark until actual files, access terms, source identities, annotations, and split lineage are reviewed and recorded. Keep downloaded media and labels outside Git.

## License and annotation handling

Roboflow's project-level license declaration does not document the complete source and rights chain for every upstream image/video. Record the exact version, attribution, upstream sources, and split ancestry in the private dataset manifest before use. Pexels clips are excluded from CrowdSight benchmarking/product evaluation absent express permission because the [Pexels Terms of Service](https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/) restrict those purposes. The Commons video is declared CC BY-SA 4.0; preserve attribution and review share-alike obligations before distributing adaptations. These are operational source checks, not legal opinions or proof of rights in people depicted.

SAM-generated masks or boxes are **pseudo-labels**. A human reviewer must correct missed, duplicate, merged, or wrong-class instances before metrics are reported. Keep source frames, pseudo-labels, reviewed labels, and predictions separate. Freeze the sampled frame list and split before model predictions. Use a new video as exploratory external evaluation only; do not call it a site-acceptance test or claim Vietnam-domain performance unless its camera and scene match the intended pilot.

3. Install the official SAM3 repository and authenticate locally with `hf auth login` after access is granted. Enter the Hugging Face token only in the local credential prompt; never paste it into chat, a project file, terminal transcript committed to Git, or `.env` tracked by Git.
4. Before annotating the full review set, run one representative frame and confirm model loading, GPU memory, and output quality. If the 4 GiB GPU cannot complete this step, use an authorized machine with more memory or another approved SAM3 runtime.
5. Export SAM proposals as candidate boxes/masks, then complete human review and adjudication in the prediction-free review workflow. Preserve model-generated and human-reviewed labels as separate artifacts.

Use the model only for annotation assistance; generated masks or boxes remain pseudo-labels until a human has checked misses, duplicates, merged people, and incorrect classes.
